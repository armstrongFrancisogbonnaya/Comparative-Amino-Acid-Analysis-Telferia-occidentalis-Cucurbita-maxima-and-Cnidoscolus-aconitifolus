# Comparative Amino Acid Analysis of Leaves of *Telferia occidentalis*, *Cucurbita maxima* and *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus*

## Objective

This research project investigated and compared the amino acid composition of the leaves of three plant species: *Telferia occidentalis*, *Cucurbita maxima*, and *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus*. The study focused on determining their nitrogen content and amino acid profiles, with particular interest in amino acids associated with nutritional value and potential anti-sickling properties.

The research involved laboratory sample preparation, defatting, nitrogen determination, acid and alkaline hydrolysis, and amino acid analysis using an Applied Biosystems PTH Amino Acid Analyzer. The study ultimately compared the amino acid profiles of the three plant extracts and evaluated their potential nutritional and anti-sickling significance.

## Steps

### 1. Sample Collection

*Telferia occidentalis* and *Cucurbita maxima* samples were obtained from Chobe Market, Jos, Plateau State, while *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus* was obtained from the University of Jos permanent site. The plant samples were identified in the Plant Science Department, Faculty of Natural Sciences, University of Jos.

The samples were dried at room temperature and subsequently homogenized into powdered form using a mortar and pestle.

*Ref 1: Collection and preparation of plant samples*

---

### 2. Sample Defatting

The powdered plant samples were defatted using a chloroform/methanol mixture at a ratio of 2:1.

Approximately 500 mg of sample was placed in an extraction thimble and subjected to Soxhlet extraction for 15 hours.

*Ref 2: Soxhlet extraction and sample defatting*

---

### 3. Nitrogen Determination

Nitrogen content was determined using the Kjeldahl method.

Approximately 115 mg of the ground sample was weighed and placed in a Kjeldahl digestion flask. Concentrated sulphuric acid and a catalyst mixture containing sodium sulphate, copper sulphate, and selenium oxide were added.

The sample was digested for approximately three hours until a light-green solution was obtained. The digest was diluted, distilled using a Markham distillation apparatus, and collected in boric acid containing an indicator. The distillate was subsequently titrated with standardized hydrochloric acid.

*Ref 3: Kjeldahl nitrogen determination*

---

### 4. Hydrolysis of Samples

For general amino acid analysis, the defatted sample was hydrolyzed using 6N hydrochloric acid.

The sample was placed in a glass ampoule, hydrochloric acid was added, and oxygen was expelled using nitrogen gas to minimize oxidation of susceptible amino acids. The ampoule was sealed and heated in an oven.

Following hydrolysis, the sample was filtered and evaporated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The residue was dissolved in acetate buffer and stored prior to analysis.

*Ref 4: Acid hydrolysis and preparation of amino acid samples*

---

### 5. Tryptophan Determination

Because tryptophan is destroyed during conventional acid hydrolysis with 6N HCl, a separate alkaline hydrolysis procedure was used.

The sample was hydrolyzed using 4.2M sodium hydroxide. After hydrolysis, the sample was neutralized to pH 7.00, evaporated under vacuum, and the residue was dissolved in borate buffer before analysis.

*Ref 5: Alkaline hydrolysis for tryptophan determination*

---

### 6. Amino Acid Analyzer

The prepared hydrolysate was loaded into the Applied Biosystems PTH Amino Acid Analyzer.

Approximately 60 microlitres of the hydrolysate was dispensed into the analyzer cartridge. The analyzer separated and analyzed acidic, neutral, and basic amino acids.

An integrator attached to the analyzer calculated the peak areas, which were proportional to the concentration of individual amino acids.

*Ref 6: Applied Biosystems PTH Amino Acid Analyzer*

---

### 7. Analysis of Essential Amino Acids

The essential amino acid profiles of the three plant extracts were compared based on their concentrations in grams per 100 grams of protein.

The analysis included:

* Leucine
* Lysine
* Isoleucine
* Phenylalanine
* Tryptophan
* Valine
* Threonine
* Arginine
* Histidine
* Methionine

The study reported that the concentrations of all the measured essential amino acids were higher in *Telferia occidentalis* compared with *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus* and *Cucurbita maxima*.

*Ref 7: Essential amino acid profile comparison*

---

### 8. Analysis of Non-Essential Amino Acids

The non-essential amino acid profiles were also compared among the three plant extracts.

The analysis included:

* Proline
* Tyrosine
* Cystine
* Alanine
* Glutamate
* Glycine
* Serine
* Aspartic acid

The highest concentrations were generally recorded in *Telferia occidentalis*, followed by *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus* and *Cucurbita maxima*.

*Ref 8: Non-essential amino acid profile comparison*

---

### 9. Results

The nitrogen contents obtained from the plant extracts were:

| Plant                      | Nitrogen Content (%) |
| -------------------------- | -------------------: |
| *Telferia occidentalis*    |         7.00 ± 0.008 |
| *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus* |         4.75 ± 0.008 |
| *Cucurbita maxima*         |         4.42 ± 0.016 |

*Telferia occidentalis* recorded the highest nitrogen content among the three plant extracts.

The amino acid results similarly showed that *Telferia occidentalis* generally had higher concentrations of both essential and non-essential amino acids.

*Ref 9: Experimental results*

---

### 10. Key Findings

The study found that:

* *Telferia occidentalis* had the highest nitrogen content.
* *Telferia occidentalis* had the highest concentrations across the measured essential amino acids.
* *Telferia occidentalis* also recorded the highest concentrations of the measured non-essential amino acids.
* Leucine, lysine, phenylalanine, and arginine were among the prominent essential amino acids identified.
* Glutamate, aspartic acid, and tyrosine were among the prominent non-essential amino acids identified.
* The study associated the presence of amino acids such as phenylalanine and arginine with potential anti-sickling properties.

*Ref 10: Comparative interpretation of amino acid results*

---

## Conclusion

The research demonstrated differences in the amino acid composition of *Telferia occidentalis*, *Cnidoscolus aconitifolus*, and *Cucurbita maxima*.

Based on the experimental results, *Telferia occidentalis* recorded the highest nitrogen content and generally showed higher concentrations of the analyzed amino acids than the other two plant extracts.

The project concluded that the three plant leaves contain amino acids considered relevant to their nutritional and potential anti-sickling properties, with *Telferia occidentalis* showing the strongest profile among the plants studied.

## Project Background

This project was completed as a Bachelor of Science (B.Sc.) research project in the Department of Biochemistry, Faculty of Medical Sciences, University of Jos, Plateau State, Nigeria, in April 2019.

The research provided practical experience in biochemical laboratory procedures, analytical sample preparation, amino acid profiling, experimental data interpretation, and scientific research documentation.
